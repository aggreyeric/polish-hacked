The Dative: "To/For Whom"

The Dative case answers the question "to whom?" or "for whom?" — it marks the indirect object. In English, you often use "to" or "for": "I gave the book to him." In Polish, you skip the preposition and just change the ending: daję mu ("I give him").

The Dative is also used in a huge number of impersonal constructions — expressions where there's no grammatical subject, just an experience: Zimno mi ("I'm cold" — literally "cold to-me"), Podoba mi się ("I like it" — literally "appeals to-me").

💡 The Invisible Subject

Many Polish feeling-expressions use Dative + adverb, with no "I" subject. You don't say Jestem zimno. You say Zimno mi ("Cold to-me"). The mi is the Dative form of ja (I). Other persons: ci (to you), mu (to him), jej (to her), nam (to us), wam (to you pl.), im (to them).

The Vocative: Calling Someone

The Vocative is the case for direct address — when you call someone's name or title. In everyday Polish, the Vocative is slowly dying; many Poles just use the Nominative even in formal address. But in formal situations — addressing a doctor, a professor, a distinguished person — the Vocative still lives.

💡 When to Use Vocative

In casual speech, just use the Nominative: "Tomasz, chodź tu!" instead of "Tomku, chodź tu!" Save the Vocative for formal address (Panie, Pani) and when you want to sound affectionate or respectful. Young Poles increasingly skip it entirely.

The Full 7-Case Map

You've now seen all seven Polish cases. Here's the one table that puts it all together — when to use each, and the one-line summary that matters:

# Case Answer to… When to Use Key Example
1 Mianownik (Nominative) Who? What? Subject of sentence — dictionary form To jest kawa.
2 Biernik (Accusative) Who? What? (object) Direct object of most verbs Mam kawę.
3 Dopełniacz (Genitive) Whose? Of what? Possession, negation, quantities, after "without" Nie mam czasu.
4 Celownik (Dative) To whom? For whom? Indirect object, impersonal feelings Daję mu. Zimno mi.
5 Narzędnik (Instrumental) With what? By means of what? "With," "I am [profession]," after certain verbs Jestem nauczycielem.
6 Miejscownik (Locative) About whom/what? Where? After prepositions w, na, o, po Mieszkam w Warszawie.
7 Wołacz (Vocative) — (direct address) Calling someone's name/title Panie! Tomku!
💡 The 5-Case Reality

You actually use 5 cases daily: Nominative, Accusative, Genitive, Instrumental, Locative. Dative appears in ~10 fixed expressions and pronouns. Vocative is for formal address only. Don't memorize all endings — learn the patterns that trigger each case (negation → Genitive, jestem + profession → Instrumental, prepositions w/na/o → Locative) and you'll absorb the rest through use.

🎯 Speaking Drill
"I'm giving a gift to my sister" — all-case exercise. Build a 1-minute monologue where you: (1) describe the gift in Nominative (To jest książka), (2) say you have it in Accusative (Mam książkę), (3) mention you're giving it in Dative (Daję siostrze), (4) describe yourself as a giver in Instrumental (Jestem bratem), (5) talk about the gift in Locative (Mówię o książce), and (6) call your sister in Vocative (Anno!>"). Try to use all 7 cases in one flowing narrative.
