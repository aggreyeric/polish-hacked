The Three Levels of "How Are You?"

Polish doesn't have one "how are you" — it has three, each for a different level of closeness. Use the wrong one and you'll sound either stiff or too familiar.

Level 1 — Casual (friends)
Co słychać?
"What's up? / What's new?" — The most common informal greeting. Literally: "What's to be heard?"
Answer: Niezły, a u ciebie? (Not bad, and you?) or just Wszystko dobrze (All good).
Level 2 — Familiar (acquaintances)
Jak leci?
"How's it going?" — Casual but slightly more relaxed than Co słychać. Literally: "How is it flying?"
Answer: Dobrze, dzięki (Good, thanks) or Jakoś leci (It's going somehow).
Level 3 — Standard (older relatives, formal-ish)
Jak się masz?
"How are you?" — The most standard version, closer to the English phrase. Works with older family members or semi-formal acquaintances.
Answer: Dobrze, dziękuję (Well, thank you).
💡 Cultural Note

Unlike English, Poles don't ask "how are you" to strangers or cashiers. It's genuinely a question, not a greeting. If someone asks you Jak się masz?, they actually want to know. Don't say "fine" and walk away — give a short real answer.

The Art of "No" Politely

In Polish culture, a blunt nie (no) can come across as rude. Poles soften refusals. Here's the spectrum from soft to firm:

Zobaczymy
/zɔˈbat͡sɨmɨ/
We'll see (the softest no)
Raczej nie
/ˈrat͡sʐɛ ɲɛ/

Niestety nie
/ɲɛˈɕtɛtɨ ɲɛ/

Nie, dziękuję
/ɲɛ dʑɛŋˈkujɛ/

Nie bardzo
/ɲɛ ˈbɔʐnɔ/

Może kiedy indziej
/ˈmɔʐɛ ˈkɔɡdɨ ˈindʐɛj/

/ˈbardzɔ mʲi ˈprɨkrɔ/

🎯 Speaking Drill
Have a 3-minute unscripted small talk conversation. Start with one of the three "how are you" levels (pick the right one for the person). Respond using at least 3 fillers (no właśnie, naprawdę?, rozumiem). Then practice a polite refusal — say "no" to an invitation without using the word nie alone. Try: "Niestety nie dam rady, może kiedy indziej?"
