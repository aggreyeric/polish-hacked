Why Polish Has Cases (And Why It's Easier Than It Looks)

Polish nouns change their endings depending on their role in the sentence. That's it. That's what "cases" are. English does this with word order ("The man sees the dog" vs "The dog sees the man"). Polish does it with word endings instead.

You already know the Nominative case — it's the dictionary form of every noun. That's what you've been learning. Now you need the Accusative — the form a noun takes when something is done to it (it's the direct object of a verb).

💡 The Only Rule That Matters

For the Accusative, there's one key split: animate masculine nouns (people, animals) get one ending, and everything else (inanimate masculine, feminine, neuter) gets another. In practice, the only big change is masculine singular. Feminine stays the same or adds -ę. Neuter is identical to nominative. You'll absorb the patterns naturally from examples.

The Pattern: Subject + Verb + Accusative Object

Every sentence in this pattern uses the Accusative on the object:

Nominative (dictionary) Accusative (object) Example Sentence English
kawa (f) kawę Mam kawę I have coffee
bilet (m) bilet Kupuję bilet I'm buying a ticket
chleb (m) chleb Jem chleb I'm eating bread
woda (f) wodę Piję wodę I'm drinking water
kot (m, animate) kota Widzę kota I see the cat
książka (f) książkę Czytam książkę I'm reading a book
samochód (m) samochód Mam samochód I have a car
dom (m) dom Widzę dom I see a house
pieniądze (pl) pieniądze Mam pieniądze I have money
czas (m) czas Mam czas I have time

Accusative Endings — Quick Reference

Gender Nominative ending Accusative ending Example
Masc. inanimate - consonant = Nominative (no change!) dom → dom
Masc. animate (people/animals) - consonant -a kot → kota, brat → brata
Masc. personal (-a) -a -ę kolega → kolegę
Feminine (-a) -a -ę kawa → kawę, woda → wodę
Feminine (-i/-y consonant) consonant = Nominative (no change!) noc → noc, mysz → mysz
Neuter -o / -e = Nominative (no change!) okno → okno, piwo → piwo

🎯 Speaking Drill
Create 15 sentences using the patterns "I have / I want / I buy" with accusative objects. Use at least 5 different nouns for each verb. Say each aloud: Mam kawę. Chcę bilet. Kupuję chleb. Mam psa. Piję wodę. Focus on the accusative ending — does it change from the dictionary form or not?
