Emotions: How You Feel

In Polish, the most natural way to say how you feel is Czuję się… ("I feel…") followed by an adjective in the instrumental case. This is your gateway into emotional vocabulary — master one pattern and you unlock dozens of expressions.

The pattern: Czuję się + adjective (instrumental). For masculine speakers the adjective ends in -y, for feminine speakers -a. But here's the good news: in everyday speech, many Poles just use the nominative form after czuję się, so you'll be understood either way.

💡 Instrumental Quick Recall

You met the instrumental in Lesson 7: "Jestem nauczycielem." The same case works here: czuję się dobrze (I feel good), czuję się źle (I feel bad). The adjective dobrze/źle is an adverb here, so it doesn't change by gender — your simplest path.

State Expressions: I'm Tired, I'm Hungry

Polish distinguishes between feeling something (emotions) and being in a state (physical conditions). Physical states use Jestem… with a adjective, or Jest mi… with an adverb.

/jɛstɛm zmɛ̃tʂɔnɨ/

Jestem głodny / głodna
/jɛstɛm ɡwɔdnɨ/

Jest mi zimno
/jɛst mi ʑimnɔ/

Jest mi gorąco
/jɛst mi ɡɔrɔnt͡sɔ/

Jest mi smutno
/jɛst mi smutnɔ/

/jɛst mi pʂɨkrɔ/

💡 Pattern Difference

Jestem + adjective (jestem zmęczony) describes your state directly and agrees with gender. Jest mi + adverb (jest mi zimno) is impersonal — no gender agreement needed. Use jest mi for physical sensations (cold, hot) and jestem for adjectives (tired, hungry).

Weather: The #1 Polish Small-Talk Topic

If there's one topic that opens every Polish conversation, it's the weather. It's not shallow — it's ritual. Poles bond over the rain, marvel at the sun, and complain about the cold. Master these phrases and you'll never be stuck for words.

Weather in Polish uses the impersonal construction — no subject, just the verb or adverb. Pada means "it's raining" literally "(it) falls." Świeci słońce means "the sun is shining." Simple, direct, no it needed.

Seasons

Seasons carry cultural weight in Poland — winter is long and dark, summer is short and precious. When talking about seasons, use w + locative: wiosną (in spring), latem (in summer), jesienią (in autumn), zimą (in winter).

💡 Culture Note

Ask any Pole about their favorite season and you'll get a real answer. Summer (lato) is cherished because it's brief. Winter (zima) is endured. Spring (wiosna) brings relief and hope. Use Jaka u was jest pogoda? ("What's the weather like with you?") to open any conversation with warmth.

🎯 Speaking Drill
2-minute monologue: "How I feel today + weather." Set a timer and talk non-stop in Polish. Start with how you feel (Czuję się…), describe the weather outside (Dzisiaj…), mention the season (Teraz jest…), and end with what you'd rather be doing. Use at least 5 emotion/weather words. Don't stop talking — fluency comes from pushing through, not pausing.
