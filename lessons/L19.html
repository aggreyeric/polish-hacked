Future Tense: Two Paths, One Meaning

Polish future tense is refreshingly simple — but it has two forms. The simpler path is będę ("I will be") + the infinitive. The more native-sounding path is będę + the past tense form (third person, gendered). Both are correct. Start with the simpler one.

Będę robić
/bɛ̃dɛ ˈrɔbʲit͡ɕ/
I will do (infinitive — simpler)
Będę robił / robiła
/bɛ̃dɛ ˈrɔbʲiw / ˈrɔbʲiwa/
I will do (past participle — m / f)
💡 Which to Choose?

Use będę + infinitive (będę robić) when you're learning — it's gender-neutral and always correct. Switch to będę + past form (będę robił/robiła) when you want to sound more native. The infinitive form is perfectly understood by every Pole; nobody will find it odd.

Future Tense in Action

Some verbs have their own simple future form — one word, no będę needed. These are mostly verbs of motion and key verbs you'll use constantly:

Pójdę
/pujdɛ/
I will go (perfective)
Zrobię
/ˈzrɔbʲɛ/
I will do / make (perfective)
Zjem
/ʑɛm/
I will eat
Zadzwonię
/zad͡zvɔɲɛ/
I will call
Napiszę
/naˈpʲiʂɛ/
I will write
Przeczytam
/pʂɨt͡ʂɨtam/
I will read
Kupię
/ˈkupʲɛ/
I will buy
Obejrzę
/ɔbɛjʐɛ/
I will watch
💡 Perfective vs Imperfective

Polish verbs come in pairs: imperfective (ongoing, habitual) and perfective (completed, one-time). iść (to go) → pójdę (I will go). robic (to do) → zrobię (I will do). The perfective form is the future — no będę needed. When in doubt, będę + infinitive always works.

Conditionals: What Would Happen

Conditionals express hypothetical scenarios — "I would go, but…" In Polish, the conditional is built with the particle by, which attaches to pronouns or conjunctions. The verb is in the past tense form.

The key pattern: Chciałbym / Chciałabym ("I would like" — m / f). You already know this from ordering food (Lesson 3). Now expand it to full conditional sentences.

Chciałbym / Chciałabym
/tʂɨawdbɨm/ / /tʂɨawdabɨm/
I would like (m / f)
Poszedłbym / Poszłabym
/pɔʂɛdwbɨm/ / /pɔʂwabɨm/
I would go (m / f)
Zrobiłbym / Zrobiłabym
/ˈzrɔbʲwbɨm/ / /ˈzrɔbʲabɨm/
I would do (m / f)
Chciałbym, ale…
/tʂɨawdbɨm ˈalɛ/
I would like to, but…
Gdybym miał czas, to bym…
/ˈɡɨbɨm maw ˈt͡sas tɔ bɨm/
If I had time, I would… (m)
Gdybym miała czas, to bym…
/ˈɡɨbɨm ˈmʲawa t͡sas tɔ bɨm/
If I had time, I would… (f)
Poszedłbym, gdyby…
/pɔʂɛdwbɨm ˈɡɨbɨ/
I would go, if… (m)
Byłoby fajnie
/bɨwabɨ ˈfajɲɛ/
It would be nice
💡 The Conditional Particle

The magic word is by — it turns a past-tense verb into a conditional. Chciałem = "I wanted." Chciałbym = "I would like." The by particle attaches to the verb ending. For feminine, add -a before: Chciałabym. For "if" clauses, gdyby = gdy (if) + by.

Conditional Mini-Dialogue

— Co będziesz robić w weekend?
"What will you do on the weekend?"

— Pójdę do kina. A ty?
"I'll go to the cinema. And you?"

— Chciałbym, ale muszę pracować.
"I would like to, but I must work."

— Gdybym miał czas, to bym też poszedł.
"If I had time, I would go too."

🎯 Speaking Drill
My plans for next year — 2-minute future-tense monologue. Talk about what you will do next year using at least 3 future-tense forms (będę + infinitive or perfective like pójdę, zrobię, kupię) and 2 conditionals (chciałbym, gdybym). Say what you would do if you had more time, more money, or fewer obligations. Keep talking — don't pause for grammar.
