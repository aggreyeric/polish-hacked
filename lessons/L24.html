Lesson 24 — Fluency Test · ~8 min

The 10-Minute Conversation

This is your final exam. Not a grammar test — a real, unscripted conversation. Here's what you should be able to do, flowing naturally from one topic to the next:

Minutes 0–2: Introductions
Who you are, where you're from, what you do
Dzień dobry, nazywam się… Jestem z… Pracuję jako…
Use greetings (Dzień dobry), "to be" (jestem), professions (pracuję jako), origin (jestem z). Formal or informal register depending on the situation.
Minutes 2–4: Small Talk
Weather, work, how are you, plans for the weekend
Co słychać? Jak leci? Pada dzisiaj? Co robisz w weekend?
Use the 3 levels of "how are you," weather talk, fillers (no właśnie, naprawdę), polite agreement/disagreement.
Minutes 4–6: Daily Life
Your routine, family, hobbies, work/study
Zazwyczaj wstaję rano… Mam rodzinę… Lubię czytać…
Use reflexive verbs (budzić się), daily routine, possessives (mój/ma), hobbies, "I like" (lubię) + instrumental.
Minutes 6–8: Opinion & Story
Give an opinion, then tell a short story
Myślę, że… To było tak: pojechaliśmy do Krakowa…
Use opinion phrases (Myślę że, Moim zdaniem), 3-part storytelling (beginning → middle → end), past tense, conjunctions (więc, potem, mimo to), evaluation (najlepsze, nigdy nie zapomnę).
Minutes 8–10: Future Plans & Close
What you'll do next, idiomatic close, goodbye
Będę podróżować… W przyszłym roku zamierzam… Do zobaczenia!
Use future tense (będę + infinitive), conditionals (gdybym miał), plans (zamierzam), closing greetings (Do widzenia, Do zobaczenia).

Self-Assessment Checklist

After your 10-minute conversation, rate yourself honestly. Fluency is not perfection — it's communication under pressure. Use this checklist:

Fluency MarkerWhat to checkScore (1–5)
SpeedDid you speak without long pauses? Did you keep the conversation flowing?
AccuracyDid cases mostly agree? Were verb endings right? Did gender match?
RangeDid you use a variety of structures? Cases, tenses, conjunctions, idioms?
ConfidenceDid you sound like you belong in the conversation? Did you initiate or only respond?
RecoveryWhen you made a mistake, did you self-correct and keep going? Or freeze?
ComprehensionDid you understand your partner's questions without asking them to repeat constantly?
Idioms & SlangDid at least 3 idioms or slang terms appear naturally?
PersistanceDid you stay in Polish the whole time, or switch to English when stuck?
Scoring Guide

32–40: Solid B2. You're conversationally fluent. Polish people will understand you and enjoy talking with you. You can travel, work, and live in Poland in Polish.
24–31: Strong B1. You're conversational. You can handle most situations, though complex topics still challenge you. You're 2-3 months of immersion from B2.
16–23: Early B1. You're functional but fragile. You can survive and connect, but conversations feel effortful. Keep practicing — the biggest gains come from daily use.
Below 16: You're at A2. Revisit Phases 2-4 (sentence frames, cases, daily vocabulary). The foundation is there — the building needs more floors.

What "Fluency" Really Means Here

Let's be honest about what you've achieved and what you haven't:

You are (likely):
B1–B2 Conversational Fluency
You can have conversations, tell stories, express opinions, navigate Poland, handle daily life.
This is real fluency. This is what "I speak Polish" means for most people.
You are not (yet):
C1–C2 Native-Level Mastery
You can't (yet) write academic essays, debate philosophy, understand every idiom, or pass as a native speaker.
That takes years of immersion. It's not the goal of this course. You can get there if you want — but B2 is already a superpower.
You can now:
All of the above
Travel, work, make friends, read news, watch TV (with subtitles), order food, argue opinions, tell stories, make jokes.
That's life in Polish. Not textbook Polish — real Polish.

Your Path Forward: The Immersion Toolkit

You've built the engine. Now you need fuel — daily input and output. Here's how to keep climbing without a textbook:

Input — Ear Training
Entertain yourself in Polish
Netflix w PL z polskimi napisami · Podcasts · YouTube · Polskie radio
Switch your Netflix to Polish audio + Polish subtitles. Listen to Polish podcasts daily (15 min minimum). Watch Polish YouTubers — not language teachers, just creators talking about stuff you like. Your brain will absorb patterns faster than any course can teach them.
Input — Reading
Read every day, even a little
News (onet.pl, wp.pl) · Books · Reddit r/poland · Comments sections
Read Polish news headlines daily (2 min). Read one article per week fully. Start with graded readers, move to YA books, then contemporary fiction. Read social media comments — they're short and full of slang.
Output — Speaking
Use it or lose it
Language exchange (Tandem, HelloTalk) · Voice memos · Self-talk · Polish friends
Find a language partner (Tandem, HelloTalk, iTalki). Talk to yourself in Polish (describe what you're doing, narrate your day). Leave yourself voice memos in Polish. If you know any Poles, talk to them. Fifteen minutes of speaking per day beats three hours once a week.
Spaced Repetition — The 300 Core Words
Anki keeps vocabulary alive forever
Anki · Quizlet · 15 minutes daily review
Download Anki (free on desktop). Create or find a Polish deck. Review the top 300 words daily for 15 minutes. Spaced repetition is the scientifically proven way to retain vocabulary long-term. Miss a day — fine. Miss a week — you're relearning, not retaining.
Daily Habit
15 minutes, no excuses
15 min input + 15 min Anki = fluency maintenance in 30 min/day
If you maintain 30 minutes of Polish per day (15 min listening/reading + 15 min Anki/review), you'll maintain and slowly improve. If you add 15 min of speaking, you'll climb to solid B2 in 3-6 months. Consistency beats intensity, every time.

🎯 Final Speaking Drill
Record a 10-minute unscripted conversation.

Find a conversation partner — a Polish friend, a language exchange partner (Tandem, HelloTalk), or even me. Set a timer for 10 minutes. Your only rule: no English, ever. If you don't know a word, describe it in Polish (circumlocution). If you make a mistake, correct yourself and keep going. Record it. Listen back. Score yourself with the checklist above.
