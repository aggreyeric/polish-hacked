How to Read Without Knowing Every Word

You will never know every word in a Polish text — not after 24 lessons, not after 240. The difference between a struggling reader and a fluent one isn't vocabulary size. It's strategy.

📖 Reading Strategy

1. Recognize cognates. Polish shares thousands of words with English via Latin and French. Informacja = information. Polityka = politics. Sport = sport. Ekonomia = economics. Kultura = culture. Technologia = technology. Nauka = science/learning. You already know more than you think.

2. Guess from context. If the headline says "Rząd przyjął nową ustawę o edukacji" and the article is about schools, you don't need to know that ustawa means "law" — the context tells you it's something the government did about education.

3. Ignore what you don't need. You're reading a weather forecast. You need the temperature and whether it'll rain. You do not need the word "zachmurzenie" — you can see it's cloudy from the icon. Skip, skip, skip.

4. Read the shapes, not the letters. Polish words look long because of prefixes and suffixes. Train your eye to grab the root: naj-szybciej = "fastest" (you know szybko = fast). Nie-rozumiem = "I don't understand." The shape carries the meaning.

🗣️ Speaking Drill
Find a short Polish news article (try gazeta.pl or onet.pl). Scan it using the four-step strategy above — don't look up every word. Then summarize what you understood in 3–5 sentences of spoken Polish. Record yourself. You don't need to be perfect — you need to extract the gist and say it back. That's real-world reading.
