Reflexive Verbs: The "się" Family

Many daily routine verbs in Polish are reflexive — they take the particle się (oneself). Think of it as "I wash myself," "I dress myself." The się goes after the verb or before it.

budzić się
/ˈbudʑitɕ ɕɛ/
to wake up
myć się
/mɨtɕ ɕɛ/
to wash (oneself)
ubierać się
/uˈbʲɛratɕ ɕɛ/
to get dressed
kąpać się
/ˈkɔmpatɕ ɕɛ/
to bathe / take a bath
czesać się
/ˈtʂɛsatɕ ɕɛ/
to comb one's hair
golić się
/ˈɡɔlitɕ ɕɛ/
to shave (oneself)
śmiać się
/ˈɕmʲatɕ ɕɛ/
to laugh
spać
/spatʲ/
to sleep (not reflexive!)

Times of Day

Polish has four main time-of-day expressions. Note: w changes meaning with the time — "in the morning" = rano (no preposition!), "at noon" = w południe.

rano
/ˈranɔ/
in the morning
w południe
/f pɔˈwudɲɛ/
at noon
wieczorem
/vʲɛˈtʂɔrɛm/
in the evening
w nocy
/f ˈnɔtsɨ/
at night
po południu
/pɔ pɔˈwudɲu/
in the afternoon
o której?
/ɔ ˈkutɔxʲi/
at what time?

Days of the Week + Months

Polish days are not capitalized (unlike English). The pattern: most days end in -da (from "to do/happen"). Months have slavic names, not Latin ones — but they're easy to learn once you know the pattern.

Frequency Adverbs: Always, Never, Sometimes

These adverbs let you describe your habits — "I always drink coffee," "I never skip breakfast." Place the adverb before the verb.

/ˈzavʂɛ/

nigdy
/ˈɲɪɡdɨ/

często
/ˈtʂɛ̃stɔ/

czasami
/tʂaˈsamʲi/
sometimes
rzadko
/ˈʐatko/
rarely
zwykle
/ˈzvɨklɛ/

Speaking Drill
Describe your typical day in 2 minutes. Use at least 5 reflexive verbs, 3 frequency adverbs, all 4 times of day, and mention what time you do key activities. Speak out loud — don't write it first.
