The Three-Part Story Structure

Every Polish story — from a weekend recap to a grandparents' war tale — follows the same three beats. Master these structural anchors and you can tell any story.

Beginning — Setting the scene
"There was…" / "Once…" / "It started when…"
Był sobie raz… / To było tak… / Zaczęło się od tego, że…
Set the time, place, and who was there. Past tense, third person.
Middle — The action unfolds
"And then…" / "Suddenly…" / "Meanwhile…"
A potem… / Nagle… / W tym czasie…
Chain events with conjunctions. This is where the story lives.
End — The payoff
"So in the end…" / "That's why…" / "And that's how…"
Więc w końcu… / Dlatego… / I tak właśnie…
Wrap it up. Give the listener the point or the punchline.

Third-Person Past Tense

You learned first-person past tense in Lesson 8 (-łem / -łam). Now you need third person — because you'll tell stories about what he did, what she did, what they did. The pattern is simple: drop the -łem/-łam, add gendered third-person endings.

PersonExample: iść (to go)Example: robić (to do)Ending pattern
on (he)poszedłzrobił-ł ( masculine)
ona (she)poszłazrobiła-ła (feminine)
ono (it)poszłozrobiło-ło (neuter)
oni (they, mixed/masc)poszlizrobili-li (masculine plural group)
one (they, fem/neuter)poszłyzrobiły-ły (feminine/neuter plural)

See the pattern? The stem stays the same. Only the ending changes for gender and number. One verb, five forms — that's all you need to narrate any story about anyone.

Expressing Evaluation: Best, Worst, Funniest

Stories need emotional coloring. Was it amazing? Terrible? Hilarious? Here's how you evaluate in Polish:

To było niesamowite
/tɔ ˈbɨ.wɔ ɲɛ.saˈmɔ.vʲi.tɛ/
It was amazing
To był najgorszy dzień
/tɔ ˈbɨw naɪˈɡɔr.sɨ ɖʑɛɲ/
It was the worst day
To było zabawne
/tɔ ˈbɨ.wɔ zaˈbav.nɛ/
It was funny
Najlepsze, co mi się przydarzyło
/naɪˈlɛp.ʂɛ tsɔ mʲi ɕɛ pʂɨˈda.rɨ.wɔ/
The best thing that happened to me
Nigdy nie zapomnę
/ˈɲiɡ.dɨ ɲɛ zaˈpɔm.ɲɛ/
I'll never forget (it)
Warto było
/ˈvar.tɔ ˈbɨ.wɔ/
It was worth it

The key prefix is naj- — it turns any adjective into the superlative. Dobry (good) → najlepszy (best). Gorszy (worse) → najgorszy (worst). Fajny (cool) → najfajniejszy (coolest, most fun).

Putting It Together: A Mini-Story

Here's what a real Polish story sounds like using everything we've learned:

Example story
"It started when we went to Kraków last weekend."
To było tak: pojechaliśmy do Krakowa w zeszły weekend.
Najpierw chcieliśmy zwiedzać zamek, ale nagle zaczął padać deszcz.

Translation: "It was like this: we went to Kraków last weekend. First we wanted to visit the castle, but suddenly it started raining."

Continuation
"So we went to a café instead, and it turned out to be the best part of the trip."
Więc poszliśmy do kawiarni zamiast tego, i to był najlepszy moment całej podróży.
Mimo to, wycieczka była super. Nigdy nie zapomnę.

Translation: "So we went to a café instead, and it was the best moment of the whole trip. Despite that (the rain), the trip was great. I'll never forget it."

Look at what's happening: To było tak sets the scene, najpierw… ale nagle drives the action, więc pivots, najlepszy evaluates, mimo to adds nuance, nigdy nie zapomnę closes. That's a full story architecture in six connectors.

🗣️ Speaking Drill
3-minute story about your most memorable day.

Use the three-part structure (beginning → middle → end). Use at least 5 conjunctions from today's lesson. Use third-person past tense for at least one person other than yourself. End with an evaluation ("It was the best/worst/funniest…"). Record yourself — aim for 3 minutes without stopping. Don't worry about mistakes — worry about keeping the story moving.
