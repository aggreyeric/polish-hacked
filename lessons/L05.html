Why Frames Beat Vocabulary

You've been learning words one at a time. That's about to end. Right now, you're going to learn six sentence frames — reusable structures where you can drop in any noun, any adjective, any verb infinitive, and instantly produce a correct Polish sentence.

Think of these as sentence templates. Frame 1 lets you describe anything. Frame 2 lets you declare possession. Frame 3 lets you express desire. Each frame is a sentence factory — you feed it words, it produces Polish.

💡 The Math

6 frames × 100 nouns = 600 possible sentences. Add 30 adjectives and you're over a thousand. This lesson is the highest ROI in the entire course.

The Six Frames

Frame 1
[Subject] is [adjective]
To jest… / On jest… / Ona jest…
"This is… / He is… / She is…" — describe anything with an adjective.
Examples: To jest dobre (This is good), On jest wysoki (He is tall), Ona jest mądra (She is smart)
Frame 2
I have / I don't have [noun]
Mam… / Nie mam…
"I have… / I don't have…" — declare possession or lack. Object goes into Accusative (positive) or Genitive (negated).
Examples: Mam kawę (I have coffee), Nie mam czasu (I don't have time), Mam samochód (I have a car)
Frame 3
I want / I would like [noun or verb]
Chcę… / Chciałbym… (m) / Chciałabym… (f)
"I want… / I would like…" — express desire. Chcę is direct; Chciałbym/Chciałabym is polite.
Examples: Chcę kawę (I want coffee), Chciałbym bilet (I would like a ticket), Chcę iść do domu (I want to go home)
Frame 4
I must / I need to [verb infinitive]
Muszę + infinitive
"I must… / I have to…" — express obligation. Just add any verb's infinitive form.
Examples: Muszę iść (I must go), Muszę pracować (I have to work), Muszę kupić bilet (I need to buy a ticket)
Frame 5
I am going to [place]
Idę do + [place in Genitive]
"I'm going to…" — announce your destination. The place goes into Genitive case after do.
Examples: Idę do sklepu (I'm going to the store), Idę do domu (I'm going home), Idę do pracy (I'm going to work)
Frame 6
Can you…? / Could you…?
Czy możesz + infinitive?
"Can you…?" — ask for help or a favor. The most powerful question frame in Polish.
Examples: Czy możesz mi pomóc? (Can you help me?), Czy możesz powtórzyć? (Can you repeat?), Czy możesz otworzyć okno? (Can you open the window?)
💡 Gender Note

Frames 3's polite form is gendered: Chciałbym (if you're male), Chciałabym (if you're female). For Frame 5, Idę is gender-neutral. For Frame 6, Czy możesz works regardless of your gender — it agrees with the listener's gender, but since it's the 2nd person singular, one form fits all.

Combining Frames

The real magic happens when you chain frames together with connectors. Here are full sentences built from the toolkit above:

Polish Frame(s) Used English
Mam kawę, ale chcę herbatę. Frame 2 + ale + Frame 3 I have coffee, but I want tea.
Muszę iść do pracy. Frame 4 + Frame 5 I have to go to work.
Nie mam czasu, więc nie mogę. Frame 2 (neg) + więc + móc I don't have time, so I can't.
Chciałbym bilet, żeby iść do kina. Frame 3 + żeby + Frame 5 I would like a ticket to go to the cinema.
Czy możesz mi pomóc? Nie mam klucza. Frame 6 + Frame 2 (neg) Can you help me? I don't have the key.
On jest inteligentny, ale też leniwy. Frame 1 + ale + też He is smart, but also lazy.
🎯 Speaking Drill
Build 20 original sentences using the six frames. For each sentence, choose a frame, drop in a noun or verb from your existing vocabulary, and say it aloud. Use at least 3 different frames. Then try combining two frames in one sentence using ale, bo, więc, or żeby.

Starter examples: Mam dom. Chcę kawę. Muszę pracować. Idę do sklepu. Czy możesz powtórzyć?
