Possessive Adjectives: "This Is My…"

In Polish, possessives change by gender. Mój = my (masculine), moja = my (feminine), moje = my (neuter).

PolishEnglishGender
To jest mój brat.This is my brother.Masculine
To jest moja siostra.This is my sister.Feminine
To jest moje dziecko.This is my child.Neuter
To jest mój ojciec.This is my father.Masculine
To jest moja mama.This is my mom.Feminine
To są moi rodzice.These are my parents.Plural (mixed)

For his/her: jego (his), jej (her), ich (their). These don't change by gender — they're invariable.

Diminutives: Why Polish Loves Them

Polish uses diminutives constantly — not just for children, but in everyday speech between adults. They express warmth, closeness, and affection. Almost every noun has a diminutive form. Here are the most common family ones:

mama → mamusia
/ˈmama → maˈmuɕa/
mommy / dear mom
tata → tatusia
/ˈtata → taˈtuɕa/
daddy / dear dad
babcia → babunia
/ˈbatɕa → baˈbuɲa/
granny / dear grandma
siostra → siostrzyczka
/ɕɔˈstra → ɕɔsˈtʂɨtʂka/
little sister / dear sister
brat → braciszek
/brat → braˈtɕiʂɛk/
little brother / dear brother
dziecko → dziecinka
/ˈdʑɛtssɔ → dʑɛˈtɕiŋka/
little child / precious child

The pattern: add -isia, -usia, -yczka, -iszek, -inka to the base word. You'll hear these everywhere — and using them makes you instantly sound more natural and warm.

Locative Case

The Locative — the "talking about" case. When you use the preposition o ("about"), the noun that follows changes to the locative case. This is how you say "I'm talking about my family."

Pattern: o + locative noun

Examples:

  • o mojej rodzinie — about my family (rodzina → rodzinie)
  • o moim bracie — about my brother (brat → bracie)
  • o mojej siostrze — about my sister (siostra → siostrze)
  • o moich rodzicach — about my parents (rodzice → rodzicach)

The locative mainly appears with o (about), w (in/at — locations), and na (on/at). You'll encounter it mostly when talking about topics or being inside places.

Speaking Drill
Introduce your family in 90 seconds. Mention at least 5 family members, use possessive adjectives correctly (mój/moja/moje), use at least one diminutive, and try using the locative case ("o mojej rodzinie…"). Speak naturally — as if talking to a new Polish friend.
