# Polish Hacked: Beginner to Fluency
## A Fast-Path Video Course — sabiedu.online

**Method:** High-frequency vocabulary + sentence patterns > grammar tables. You speak from Lesson 1.
- Top 300 words = ~70% of daily Polish conversation
- 6 sentence frames + 2 tenses = you can say almost anything
- Cases learned as patterns, not paradigms — you absorb them *using* them, not memorizing tables
- Each lesson = one video (~5–8 min) + one speaking drill + one vocab pack

**Total:** 24 lessons across 6 phases · ~300 words · full conversational fluency

---

## PHASE 1 — SURVIVAL (Lessons 1–4)
*Goal: You can greet, introduce yourself, order food, and survive a day in Poland.*

### Lesson 1 — Sounds of Polish: The 8 Sounds English Doesn't Have
- The sibilant minefield: sz/ś, cz/ć, ż/rz/ź/dź/dż
- Nasal vowels: ą, ę (and why nobody actually nasalizes them fully)
- The rolling R, the dark L (ł = "w")
- Minimal pairs drill: we pronounce vs. you pronounce
- **Vocab:** 20 survival words (cześć, tak, nie, proszę, dziękuję, przepraszam)
- **Speaking drill:** Record yourself saying 10 words, compare to native

### Lesson 2 — Hello, I Am…: Your First 50 Sentences
- Greetings hierarchy: formal (Dzień dobry) vs. informal (Cześć) — when to use which
- "I am / You are / He is" — the only verb you need for 50 sentences
- Introducing yourself: name, nationality, profession
- Question words: kto? co? gdzie? (who? what? where?)
- **Vocab:** 30 words (I, you, he/she, be, name, from, live, work, student, American/English)
- **Speaking drill:** 1-minute self-introduction recording

### Lesson 3 — Numbers, Money & Coffee: Order Without Dying
- Numbers 1–100 (the fast way — pattern, not memorization)
- "I would like…" (Chciałbym/Chciałabym) — your most useful phrase
- Ordering food and drink: coffee, tea, water, beer, the basics
- "How much?" (Ile?) + understanding the answer
- Polish restaurant script: from entrance to paying
- **Vocab:** 25 words (numbers, coffee, tea, water, bill, please, how much)
- **Speaking drill:** Roleplay ordering 3 items at a café

### Lesson 4 — Where Is…? Navigate a Polish City
- Locations: here, there, where, left, right, straight
- "Where is…?" (Gdzie jest…?) + essential places (station, toilet, hotel, pharmacy)
- Transport: ticket, bus, tram, train, taxi
- Time basics: what time, today, tomorrow, now, later
- Emergency phrases: "I need help" (Potrzebuję pomocy)
- **Vocab:** 25 words (where, station, toilet, left, right, ticket, now, help)
- **Speaking drill:** Ask for directions to 3 places

## PHASE 2 — PATTERNS (Lessons 5–8)
*Goal: You stop memorizing individual words and start building sentences with reusable frames.*

### Lesson 5 — The 6 Sentence Frames That Unlock Everything
- Frame 1: "[Subject] is [adjective]" — To jest… / On jest…
- Frame 2: "I have / I don't have" — Mam / Nie mam
- Frame 3: "I want / I would like" — Chcę / Chciałbym
- Frame 4: "I must / I need to" — Muszę + infinitive
- Frame 5: "I am going to…" — Idę do + place
- Frame 6: "Can you…?" / "Czy możesz…?"
- With these 6 frames + 100 nouns = you can generate hundreds of unique sentences
- **Vocab:** 20 connector/function words (have, want, must, can, go, to, because, but, and, or)
- **Speaking drill:** Build 20 original sentences using the 6 frames

### Lesson 6 — The Case System, Hacked (Part 1: Nominative → Accusative)
- Why Polish has cases and why it's less scary than it looks
- The Accusative (the "doing something to something" case)
- Pattern: subject + verb + accusative object (Mam kawę / Kupuję bilet)
- The only rule that matters: animate masculine vs. everything else
- 50 nouns in accusative — learn them *in sentences*, not tables
- **Vocab:** 30 nouns with their accusative forms
- **Speaking drill:** 15 "I have/I want/I buy" sentences with accusative objects

### Lesson 7 — The Case System, Hacked (Part 2: Genitive & Instrumental)
- Genitive (the "of / from / lack of" case) — Negation flips to genitive! (Nie mam czasu)
- Instrumental (the "with / by means of" case) — "I am a teacher" = Jestem nauczycielem
- The 2 patterns you need:
  1. Negative + verb → genitive (Nie mam pieniędzy, Nie chcę kawy)
  2. "I am [profession]" → instrumental (Jestem lekarzem, Jestem inżynierem)
- **Vocab:** 20 nouns in genitive + 15 professions in instrumental
- **Speaking drill:** 10 negative sentences + 10 "I am" sentences

### Lesson 8 — Past Tense: The Only Shortcut in Polish
- Past tense is actually *easier* than present — one pattern, applies to all verbs
- Gender agreement: I said (m) vs. I said (f) — mówilam vs. mówiłem
- The -łem/-łam ending = "I did" (almost universal)
- Talking about yesterday: what you did, where you went, what you ate
- **Vocab:** 20 common verbs in past tense
- **Speaking drill:** 2-minute "what I did yesterday" mini-story

## PHASE 3 — CONNECT (Lessons 9–12)
*Goal: You can hold a 5-minute conversation about daily life, family, and work.*

### Lesson 9 — Small Talk Like a Pole
- "How are you?" — the 3 levels (Co słychać? / Jak leci? / Jak się masz?)
-天气/Work/Family small-talk scripts
- 10 conversation fillers that make you sound natural (no więc, właśnie, naprawdę?)
- The art of "no" politely (nie, niestety, raczej nie, zobaczymy)
- **Vocab:** 25 conversation bricks
- **Speaking drill:** 3-minute unscripted small talk

### Lesson 10 — Your Day, Your Life: Daily Routine
- Reflexive verbs: to wake up, to wash, to get dressed (budzić się, myć się, ubierać się)
- Times of day: rano, w południe, wieczorem, w nocy
- Days of the week + months (the pattern is simpler than you think)
- "I usually…" vs "I never…" vs "I always…"
- Present tense verbs summary — the 4 conjugation patterns, compressed
- **Vocab:** 30 daily-life words
- **Speaking drill:** Describe your typical day in 2 minutes

### Lesson 11 — Family & Relationships
- Family vocabulary (matka, ojciec, brat, siostra, babcia, dziadek)
- "This is my…" (To jest mój/ma…) — possessive adjectives
- Talking about age, marital status, children
- Diminutives: why Polish loves them (mama, tata, mamusia, tata → everything cuter)
- The Locative case (the "talking about" case) — "o" + locative (o mojej rodzinie)
- **Vocab:** 25 family + relationship words
- **Speaking drill:** Introduce your family in 90 seconds

### Lesson 12 — Work, Study & Hobbies: Tell Me About Yourself
- Professions + workplaces (szkoła, biuro, szpital, uniwersytet)
- "I work as…" + "I study…" (Pracuję jako… / Studiuję…)
- Hobbies: reading, cooking, sports, music, travel — with "I like / I enjoy" (lubię / uwielbiam)
- "I'm interested in…" (Interesuję się + instrumental)
- Putting it together: a full self-introduction (name, origin, job, hobbies, family)
- **Vocab:** 30 work/study/hobby words
- **Speaking drill:** Full 3-minute self-introduction

## PHASE 4 — FUNCTION (Lessons 13–16)
*Goal: You can handle real situations — shopping, health, phone calls, appointments.*

### Lesson 13 — Shopping & Transactions
- Colors, sizes, clothing vocabulary
- "I'm looking for…" (Szukam + genitive)
- "Do you have…?" (Czy macie…?)
- "Can I try it on?" (Czy mogę przymierzyć?)
- Prices, discounts, "too expensive", "cheaper"
- The Genitive case in the wild — quantities and "I want [amount] of [thing]"
- **Vocab:** 30 shopping words
- **Speaking drill:** Roleplay a clothing store purchase

### Lesson 14 — Health & The Pharmacy
- Body parts (głowa, brzuch, gardło, ręka, noga)
- "I feel bad / I'm sick" (Źle się czuję / Jestem chory)
- Symptoms: ból głowy, gorączka, kaszel, katar
- At the doctor: "Where does it hurt?" (Gdzie boli?)
- Pharmacy script: "I need something for…" (Potrzebuję czegoś na + accusative)
- **Vocab:** 25 health/medical words
- **Speaking drill:** Pharmacy roleplay: describe symptoms + request medicine

### Lesson 15 — Phone Calls & Appointments
- Phone opener: "Halo?" vs. English "Hello?" — why you need to know this
- "I'm calling about…" (Dzwonię w sprawie…)
- Making an appointment: date, time, confirming
- "Can I speak to…?" (Czy mogę rozmawiać z…?)
- Numbers in context: phone numbers, dates (Polish date format)
- **Vocab:** 20 phone/appointment phrases
- **Speaking drill:** Roleplay booking a doctor's appointment by phone

### Lesson 16 — Transportation, Travel & Directions
- Asking for + understanding directions (skręć w lewo, prosto, na prawo)
- Buying tickets: bus, tram, train, plane — the vocab overlap
- "How do I get to…?" (Jak dojechać do…? + genitive)
- Car vocabulary: fuel, parking, GPS, speed
- Past tense travel story: "I went to Kraków last weekend"
- **Vocab:** 30 travel/transport words
- **Speaking drill:** Give directions to your house from the nearest station

## PHASE 5 — DEPTH (Lessons 17–20)
*Goal: You stop translating in your head and start *thinking* in Polish. Expressing opinions, feelings, and nuance.*

### Lesson 17 — Opinions, Agreement & Argument
- "I think that…" / "In my opinion…" (Myślę, że… / Moim zdaniem…)
- Agreeing: "True", "Exactly", "You're right" (Prawda, dokładnie, masz rację)
- Disagreeing politely: "I don't think so", "Not necessarily" (Nie sądzę, nie koniecznie)
- "But", "however", "on the other hand" (ale, jednak, z drugiej strony)
- Modal verbs: powinienem (I should), mógłbym (I could), muszę (I must)
- **Vocab:** 20 opinion/argument phrases
- **Speaking drill:** 2-minute opinion monologue on a topic you care about

### Lesson 18 — Feelings, Emotions & Weather (The Small-Talk Twins)
- Emotions: happy, sad, angry, tired, excited, stressed, bored
- "I feel…" + instrumental (Czuję się… + instrumental)
- Weather: the #1 Polish small-talk topic (pada, świeci słońce, wietrznie, chłodno)
- "I'm tired/hungry/cold/hot" — Polish state expressions (jestem zmęczony, jestem głodny)
- Seasons and their cultural weight in Poland
- **Vocab:** 25 emotion + weather words
- **Speaking drill:** 2-minute "How I feel today + weather" monologue

### Lesson 19 — Future Tense & Conditionals: What Will/Would Happen
- Future tense = "to be" (być) in future + infinitive OR past tense particle
- Będę robić vs. Będę robił — both correct, one simpler
- "I will go / I will eat / I will call you tomorrow"
- Conditionals: "I would like to" → "If I had time, I would…" (Gdybym miał czas, to bym…)
- "I would have, but…" (Chciałbym, ale…)
- **Vocab:** 20 future/conditional structures
- **Speaking drill:** "My plans for next year" — 2-minute future-tense monologue

### Lesson 20 — Case System, Hacked (Part 3: Dative & Vocative)
- Dative (the "to/for whom" case) — "I give him" (Daję mu…), "It's cold to me" (Zimno mi)
- Vocative (the "calling someone" case) — officially dying but alive in formal address
- When you actually need each case (spoiler: Vocative only for names/titles, Dative for ~10 verbs)
- Full case summary: the 5 cases you actually use + the one you ignore
- **Vocab:** 20 words in dative/vocative
- **Speaking drill:** "I'm giving a gift to my sister" scenario using all cases

## PHASE 6 — FLUENCY (Lessons 21–24)
*Goal: You can tell stories, understand native speech, watch Polish media, and handle unexpected topics.*

### Lesson 21 — Storytelling: Tell Me About Your Weekend
- Narrative structure: beginning (There was…), middle (And then…), end (So in the end…)
- Conjunctions: potem (then), więc (so), dlatego (that's why), mimo to (despite that)
- Past tense extended: third person story (On poszedł, ona zobaczyła, oni zrobili)
- "It was the best / worst / funniest" — expressing evaluation
- **Vocab:** 20 storytelling connectors + narrative phrases
- **Speaking drill:** 3-minute story about your most memorable day

### Lesson 22 — Understanding Real Polish: Idioms, Slang & Spoken Speed
- Top 20 Polish idioms that everyone actually uses (nie moja bajka, robić z igły widły, lecieć z tematem)
- Slang: young people's Polish (spoko, mega, ogon, lipa, siara)
- Spoken vs. written Polish — what they drop in real speech (pronoun dropping, case reduction)
- Understanding native speed: listening drill with accelerated audio
- **Vocab:** 25 idioms + slang terms
- **Speaking drill:** Use 5 idioms correctly in conversation

### Lesson 23 — Reading Polish: News, Social Media & Signs
- Scanning a Polish news article: how to extract meaning without knowing every word
- Signs you'll see everywhere (nie pal, wyjście, uwaga, bilety, zabronione)
- Social media Polish: comments, memes, abbreviations
- Reading strategy: recognize cognates, guess from context, ignore what you don't need
- **Vocab:** 20 media/signs words
- **Speaking drill:** Summarize a short Polish news article in your own words

### Lesson 24 — Fluency Test: The 10-Minute Conversation
- Full simulated conversation covering: introduction → small talk → daily life → opinions → a story → future plans
- Self-assessment checklist: fluency markers (speed, accuracy, range, confidence)
- What "fluency" really means at this level: B1-B2 conversational, not C2 native
- Your path forward: immersion input (Netflix PL, podcasts, YouTube), language exchange, reading
- Polyglot's toolkit: Anki decks, spaced repetition for the 300 core words, daily 15-min speaking habit
- **Speaking drill:** Record a 10-minute unscripted conversation (with me or a partner)
