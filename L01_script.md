# Lesson 1 — Sounds of Polish: The 8 Sounds English Doesn't Have
## Polish Hacked · sabiedu.online

**Duration:** ~6 minutes
**Format:** sabiedu on camera + motion graphics (pronunciation diagrams, vocab cards)
**Goal:** By the end, the learner can pronounce all Polish sounds and recognize them in speech.

---

## SCRIPT

### BEAT 1 — HOOK [0:00–0:15]
**Visual:** sabiedu, tight close-up, dark studio, warm key light. Title card: "Lesson 1 — Sounds of Polish"

> "Polish looks terrifying. Seven consonants in a row, dots and lines over letters, sounds you've never heard. But here's the truth — there are only eight sounds in Polish that don't exist in English. Master those eight, and you can pronounce every word in the language. Let's hack them, one by one."

### BEAT 2 — THE SIBILANT MINEFIELD [0:15–1:30]
**Visual:** sabiedu speaking, motion graphics show each sound pair with mouth diagram + example word

> "Polish has two families of hissing sounds — the hard ones and the soft ones. The hard ones: SZ, CZ, Ż, DŻ. These are deep, retroflex sounds — like you're shushing a cat."

**Vocab card animation:** 
- SZ = /ʂ/ (like "sh" but deeper) — example: szafa (wardrobe)
- CZ = /tʂ/ (like "ch" but harder) — example: czas (time)
- Ż = /ʐ/ (like "s" in "measure" but harder) — example: żaba (frog)
- DŻ = /dʐ/ (like "j" in "judge" but harder) — example: dżem (jam)

> "Now the soft ones — Ś, Ć, Ź, DŹ. These are lighter, higher, almost whispering through your teeth. Think of them as the gentle versions."

**Vocab card animation:**
- Ś = /ɕ/ (soft "sh") — example: śnieg (snow)
- Ć = /tɕ/ (soft "ch") — example:ćma (moth)
- Ź = /ʑ/ (soft "zh") — example: źle (badly)
- DŹ = /dʑ/ (soft "j") — example: dźwięk (sound)

> "The trick? Hard sounds curl your tongue back. Soft sounds push it forward behind your teeth. Practice: szafa — śnieg. Czas — ćma. Żaba — źle."

### BEAT 3 — RZ: THE IMPOSTER [1:30–2:15]
**Visual:** sabiedu, motion graphics: RZ highlighted, comparison with Ż

> "Here's a sound that trips everyone up — RZ. It looks like it should be 'rz' like in the English word 'razor.' It's not. RZ is pronounced exactly the same as Ż — that deep 'zh' sound. The same deep hiss."

**Vocab card:**
- RZ = /ʐ/ (same as Ż!) — example: rzeka (river), morze (sea)

> "So when you see RZ, just say Ż. When you see Ż, say the same thing. They're identical. Two spellings, one sound. That's Polish for you — but it means less to memorize, not more."

### BEAT 4 — THE NASAL VOWELS: Ą AND Ę [2:15–3:15]
**Visual:** sabiedu, motion graphics showing nose + mouth, the nasal vowels

> "Two letters with tails — Ą and Ę. These are nasal vowels, sounds that go through your nose. But here's the secret: in everyday speech, most Poles barely nasalize them. Ą at the end of a word sounds almost like 'om.' Ę in the middle sounds like 'en.'"

**Vocab card:**
- Ą = /ɔ̃/ → sounds like "om" — example: mogą (they can), word "thanks": dziękuję (listen for the ę!)
- Ę = /ɛ̃/ → sounds like "en" — example: ręka (hand)

> "Don't obsess over the nasality. Just say 'om' for Ą and 'en' for Ę, and you'll be understood. You'll pick up the nasal resonance naturally as you listen to natives."

### BEAT 5 — Ł: THE DARK L [3:15–4:00]
**Visual:** sabiedu, motion graphics: Ł vs L comparison

> "This one surprises every English speaker. The letter Ł — a crossed L — is pronounced like the English 'W.' Not 'L,' not 'Lu.' Just 'W.'"

**Vocab card:**
- Ł = /w/ (like English "w") — example: łapa (paw), ładny (pretty), and the famous: Wrocław (Vro-tsuaf)

> "So 'Wrocław' is 'Vrotsuaf.' 'Łapa' is 'Wapa.' Once you know Ł = W, dozens of place names suddenly make sense."

### BEAT 6 — THE ROLLING R [4:00–4:30]
**Visual:** sabiedu demonstrating, motion graphics: tongue trill diagram

> "Polish R is rolled. Not the soft English R — a short trill, like two taps of your tongue against the roof of your mouth. Think of the Spanish R but shorter."

**Vocab card:**
- R = /r/ (rolled) — example: rzeka (river), rower (bike), radio

> "Don't stress if you can't roll it perfectly on day one. A single tap R is close enough for people to understand you. Practice with 'rower' — rower, rower, rower."

### BEAT 7 — THE SURVIVAL 20 [4:30–5:30]
**Visual:** sabiedu, full-screen vocab cards with pronunciation

> "Now let's put these sounds to work. Here are your first twenty survival words. Repeat each one after me."

**Vocab cards (5 seconds each):**
1. Cześć — /tʂɛɕtɕ/ — "Hi / Bye" (informal)
2. Tak — /tak/ — "Yes"
3. Nie — /ɲɛ/ — "No" (note: NI = soft N, like "canyon")
4. Proszę — /prɔʂɛ/ — "Please / You're welcome / Here you go"
5. Dziękuję — /dʑɛŋkujɛ/ — "Thank you"
6. Przepraszam — /pʃɛpraʂam/ — "Excuse me / Sorry"
7. Dzień dobry — /dʑɛɲ dɔbri/ — "Good morning/afternoon"
8. Do widzenia — /dɔ vʲidʑɛɲa/ — "Goodbye" (formal)
9. Tak jest — /tak jɛst/ — "That's right"
10. Nie ma — /ɲɛ ma/ — "There isn't / I don't have"
11. Gdzie — /ɡʑɛ/ — "Where"
12. Kto — /kto/ — "Who"
13. Co — /tso/ — "What"
14. Dobrze — /dɔbʐɛ/ — "Good / OK"
15. Źle — /ʑlɛ/ — "Bad / badly"
16. Teraz — /tɛras/ — "Now"
18. Pomożesz? — /pɔmɔʐɛʂ/ — "Will you help?"
19. Nie rozumiem — /ɲɛ rɔzumjɛm/ — "I don't understand"
20. Mówisz po angielsku? — /muvʲiʂ pɔ aŋɡʲɛlsku/ — "Do you speak English?"

> "Twenty words. That's your ammunition for day one. Twenty words plus the sounds we just learned = you can walk into any Polish conversation and survive."

### BEAT 8 — SPEAKING DRILL + CLOSE [5:30–6:00]
**Visual:** sabiedu, leaning forward, motion graphics: "Speaking Drill" card

> "Your drill: record yourself saying all twenty words. Don't read them — say them from memory if you can. Compare to the pronunciation guide. Don't worry about perfection — worry about being understood. That's the bar, and it's lower than you think."

> "Next lesson: your first fifty sentences. We learn the one verb that unlocks fifty things you can say — immediately. Cześć!"

**End card:** "Lesson 2 — Hello, I Am…: Your First 50 Sentences — Coming Soon — sabiedu.online"
